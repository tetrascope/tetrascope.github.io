    Documentation

    Open the full manual — scientific background (the basalt tetrahedron, thermal divides, O'Hara's CMAS reduction, projection geometry and mathematics), how to read each diagram, the CIPW norm, classification, validation against Yoder & Tilley's Table 2 and O'Hara's own figures, and good practice.

    1. Enter a composition

    Type oxide weight per cent (or load an example / a Table 2 analysis). Give FeO and Fe2O3 separately, or total iron as FeO* — then the Fe2O3/ΣFe ratio splits it. Negative, non-numeric or non-finite values are rejected; a total outside 97–102 wt% is accepted with a warning.

    2. Read where it sits

    The composition is reduced to O'Hara's C, M, A, S components and to the Di–Ol–Ne–Qz normative tetrahedron (via a CIPW norm). The right-hand panel names its Yoder & Tilley group and which side of each dividing plane it lies on.

    3. Project it

    Choose a preset diagram, or build any projection: one projection point and three target vertices gives a ternary; two projection points and two vertices gives a binary section. Choices where the projection point lies in the target plane are refused with a reason; nearly-coplanar choices are flagged as ill-conditioned.

    4. Plot datasets

    Load Yoder & Tilley's Table 2, paste or upload a CSV, or plot your saved collection. Export the diagram as SVG/PNG and the coordinates as CSV.

    Units

    Plotted coordinates are weight per cent of the target vertices, as in O'Hara's figures. The "formula units" column gives the same point in molecular units; the projection-point coefficient says how much of that phase must be added or subtracted to reach the plane.

    Projection (from a point)
    The composition is moved along the straight line through the projection phase until it meets the target plane; the meeting point is plotted. Equivalent to adding or subtracting that phase.
    CMAS
    The system CaO–MgO–Al2O3–SiO2. O'Hara (1968) reduces natural analyses to its four components so experiments on rocks can be compared with the synthetic system.
    C, M, A, S
    O'Hara's components: e.g. C = (CaO − 10/3 P2O5 + 2Na2O + 2K2O) × 56.08, from molecular proportions.
    Basalt tetrahedron
    Yoder & Tilley's generalised normative tetrahedron with vertices clinopyroxene (Di), olivine, nepheline and quartz.
    Critical plane of silica undersaturation
    Cpx–Ol–Pl (Di–Ol–An in CMAS). Separates nepheline-normative alkali basalts from tholeiites; a thermal divide at low pressure.
    Plane of silica saturation
    Cpx–Opx–Pl. Separates quartz-normative from olivine-normative tholeiites.
    CIPW norm
    Recalculation of an analysis into standard anhydrous minerals (Cross, Iddings, Pirsson & Washington). Used here to place rocks in the normative tetrahedron.
    Double projection / binary section
    Projection from two phases at once onto a join; gives one ratio (e.g. CS/A after removing olivine and orthopyroxene).
    Condition number
    How strongly analytical errors are amplified by a projection. Presets are below 20; values above 1000 trigger a warning.

    Please cite the original sources of the method:

    O'Hara, M. J. (1968). The bearing of phase equilibria studies in synthetic and natural systems on the origin and evolution of basic and ultrabasic rocks. Earth-Science Reviews, 4, 69-133.

Yoder, H. S. & Tilley, C. E. (1962). Origin of basalt magmas: an experimental study of natural and synthetic rock systems. Journal of Petrology, 3, 342-532.
